1.1 Purpose of this document
1.2 Document structure
1.3 Referenced documents
1.4 Naming Conventions
This specification describes the product requirements for the "Safe remote I/O" project. The specification contains all functional
and non-functional properties of the product. The requirements are taken from the document [IR] or derived, further detailed
and completed.
Due to the project structure, the complete specification of the functional and non-functional properties of individual
subcomponents is outsourced to separate component specifications.
The requirements specified in this document are the basis for the customer acceptance to be carried out at the end of the
development. The specification must therefore be approved by the customer. Changes or extensions of the requirements after
release require mutual agreement and will be recorded in a new revision of this document.
Safety Category: QM/SIL
For the product level the safety category is meant as signal that this workitem is important.
The correct usage in terms of functional safety will start from system level.
The Product Requirement Specification (PRS) is split into several sub documents to keep the necessary overhead low, if changes
are necessary only the appropriate sub-document need to be revised.
The documents are hosted within the “Polarion” software (ifm-environment) for revision management. 
Referenced documents (input documents) are documents that contain requirements or data that are reused, clarified or
supplemented in this document. Changes in these documents can therefore have an influence on this document. Therefore, the
specification of the date and/or version is mandatory for higher-level documents.

2 Introduction and Overview
2.1 Module Variants
The overarching goal is the development of a product family of safe Remote- IO Devices [SRIO].
The product family might have DI-only, DO-only or mixed DI/DO Variants with different safety-protocols to either:
transfer the input signal using the safety-protocol to,
and/or switch the digital outputs based on the received information from the F-Host.
In general the device supports various Ethernet based fieldbus protocols, the fieldbus interface also enables the customer to
connect via an IoT interface to the device for firmware updates or gather a insight in device information or statuses (only none
safety related functionalities).

8 Input & Output
8.1 Numbering of Ports and DIs/DOs
8.2 DI/DO Number Mapping
A mapping is needed between a given DI/DO number and which connector at the hardware level is denoted by this DI/DO
number. In this chapter the DI/DO number mapping is described.
For ports and DIs/DOs the following numbering is applied:
1. Port Input and Output connections of the SRIO are called ports.
The SRIO has 8 of theses ports, each connected to an M12-connector.
A port can be an input or an output port, depending on the SRIO-version. The name of one port is: Port n (n: X1..X8).
Figure 2 DI/DO Port Number
2. Channels (port-related numbering)
Each port has two channels:
Port-channel 1 and Port-channel 2
Globally they will be counted continuesly as long as the port continues the same functionality:
e.g. Port 1 and Port 2 are Digital Inputs, therefore Port 1 has Channel 1 and 2 and Port 2 has Channel 3 and 4
The mapping of port-channel 1 and port-channel 2 to the respective connector pin is shown in 
 SRIO-719
- Missing cross-reference and in 
 SRIO-717 - Missing cross-reference.
SRIO-2060 - DI/DO Numbering, DI/DO Device

This chapter explains the term "own safe address". The details about setting and using the own safe address are described in
more detail in 
 130_Communication.
In a safe fieldbus communication the communication partners identify each other via their safe addresses. When the safe
fieldbus master generates a safe fieldbus telegram the safe fieldbus master codes the safe address of the intended recipient into
the safe telegram. When the safe fieldbus slave receives a safe telegram the safe fieldbus slave checks whether the telegram is
really meant for him. For this check the safe fieldbus slave needs the following information:
1. who is the intended recipient of the telegram? This information is contained in the received telegram.
2. what is my own safe address? Typical solutions for providing this information to the safe fieldbus slave are:
- own safe address is stored in non-volatile memory in the safe fieldbus slave
- own safe address is coded via rotary switches or a DIL switch of the safe fieldbus slave
Please note the difference between the safe fieldbus parameter "slave's safe fieldbus address" (chapter 
 SRIO-1952 - Safe
Fieldbus Parameters) and "own safe address".
