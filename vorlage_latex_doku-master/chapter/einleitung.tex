\chapter{Introduction} 
\label{chap:introduction} 


Industrial automation systems increasingly connect their \gls{acr:OT} to \gls{acr:IT} networks \autocite[2, 9]{EHIE2020103166}. This convergence turns components at the boundary between the two domains into critical assets. Industrial gateways occupy exactly this boundary, translating and forwarding data between fieldbus protocols and higher-level services. Consequently, they expose previously closed \gls{acr:OT} environments to threats originating in connected \gls{acr:IT} networks \autocite[199]{8586807} \cite[p. 33]{enisa2018iot_smart_manufacturing}.



Before the new Regulation, gateway validation focused on functional behaviour and safety. Cybersecurity was not mandatory. However, Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 introduces binding cybersecurity requirements, treating protection against malicious third parties as a prerequisite for machinery safety \autocite[5]{EU2023_1230}. Functional safety can therefore no longer be evaluated independently of cybersecurity \autocite{carl2026_maschinenverordnung}. For gateways influencing safety-related functions, security properties become vital evidence for conformity assessments \cite[pp. 1]{tuv_functional_safety_cybersecurity_faq_pdf}. This Regulation is complemented by the draft standard prEN 50742 and the \gls{acr:IEC} 62443 series, which defines component-level security requirements for \gls{acr:ICS} \autocite{prEN50742_2025, IEC62443-4-2}.


Although the regulatory expectations are now explicit, the established validation process does not yet meet them. Existing functional and safety validation verifies the intended behaviour of a device, but it does not systematically verify its security functions. This creates a gap between the regulatory expectations on the one hand and the currently established methodology for functional testing on the other. The central problem addressed in this thesis is therefore the absence of a systematic approach for the functional testing of security in industrial gateways at the physical \gls{acr:IT}/\gls{acr:OT} interface.

The task of this thesis is to address this gap for a representative device, a \acrfull{acr:SRIO} device. The \gls{acr:SUT} is assessed as a safety-related component integrated into a machine. Its requirements are therefore derived component-level requirements. The primary objective is to research security testing approaches, frameworks, and organisational practices, and to develop a systematic testing methodology that verifies the security functions of such a gateway and maintains traceability between machine-level requirements and concrete test activities. The scientific contribution is the systematic derivation, justification, and partial validation of this methodology, rather than the exhaustive execution of every possible test. The methodology is demonstrated on a single device in an isolated laboratory. Within this demonstration, the practical validation is limited to the direct-access position and to a subset of the specified tests. The work therefore provides a partial empirical demonstration rather than a complete conformity assessment.

The work is guided by three research questions. First, how can the machine-level cybersecurity obligations of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 be translated into verifiable, derived component-level security tests for a safety-related industrial gateway? Second, which tests of the existing security company framework are applicable to an industrial gateway, and how can non-applicable tests be identified before execution? Third, what can direct-access testing at the physical \gls{acr:IT}/\gls{acr:OT} interface verify about the effectiveness of the implemented security functions, and where are its boundaries?

Methodologically, the thesis follows \acrfull{acr:DSRM} to create and evaluate a testing methodology artefact \autocite[54]{Peffers}. This methodology derives security tests in three stages. It extracts machine-level obligations from the Machinery Regulation, maps them via prEN 50742 to \gls{acr:IEC} 62443-4-2 component requirements, and links them to assets and threats using \gls{acr:STRIDE} principles. It then defines a test catalogue, selects techniques, and integrates an existing company framework where applicable. Demonstrated on a real safety gateway, the results yield a traceable methodology that facilitates the practical application of regulatory requirements in manufacturing and supplies necessary evidence for technical documentation.

The thesis is structured as follows. Chapter \ref{chap:fundamentals} establishes the technical, regulatory, and methodological foundations. Chapter \ref{chap:methodology} develops the testing methodology, detailing requirement derivation, traceability, and verification models. Chapter \ref{chap:implementation_results} applies this methodology to the \gls{acr:SRIO} device, reports the evidence, and evaluates the approach against the thesis objective. Finally, Chapter \ref{chap:summary_outlook} summarises the findings and provides an outlook on future work.
