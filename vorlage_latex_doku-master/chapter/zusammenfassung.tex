\chapter{Summary and Outlook} 
\label{chap:summary_outlook}

\section{Summary of Findings} 
\label{sec:summary_findings} 
%TODO
This thesis addressed the absence of a systematic approach for verifying the security functions of industrial gateways at the physical IT/OT interface. With Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, cybersecurity became a mandatory part of the conformity assessment of machinery, yet the established functional and safety validation process did not verify security functions in a requirement-driven manner. The goal was to develop, and as far as the available laboratory allowed to validate, a systematic and standards-based testing methodology that supports this verification and that can be reused for future product releases. Three research questions guided the work. RQ1 asked how the regulatory obligations can be translated into verifiable, component-level tests. RQ2 asked which tests of the existing company framework are applicable to an embedded gateway and how non-applicable tests can be identified before execution. RQ3 asked what direct-access testing at the IT/OT interface can verify about the effectiveness of the implemented security functions, and where its boundaries lie.

The methodology was developed with the Design Science Research Methodology. It combines three elements. The first is a requirement derivation that maps the obligations of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 through the draft standard prEN 50742 to the component requirements of IEC 62443-4-2. The second is a controlled reuse of the company test framework, filtered by a tool-to-target applicability assessment. The third is the verification taxonomy of IEC 62443-4-1 Practice SVV. Each requirement was linked to the assets it applies to, to a STRIDE threat, and to the corresponding security function. Each test case was specified on two levels, a product-neutral abstract scenario and a concrete, environment-specific execution. The methodology was demonstrated on a real Safe Remote I/O device in an isolated laboratory, under the direct-access network position NV1.

The derivation produced fourteen security requirements from Annex III of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 and mapped each of them to prEN 50742 and to IEC 62443-4-2. The device was decomposed into nine assets with their protection objectives. The applicability assessment classified the ten company tests as five applicable, four not applicable, and one blocked. The tests for network segmentation, default credentials, TLS configuration, and hardening validation were not applicable, because the embedded device exposes no second network zone, no authentication endpoint, no TLS endpoint, and no operating-system shell. The known-CVE scan was blocked, because the scanner could not be brought into operation within the laboratory. Of the three network access variants, only NV1 was realised. Fifteen test activities were executed under NV1, and their evidence was stored. Two of them produced a definitive requirement outcome, and both are documented device deviations: the intervention log carries only a relative system tick and no absolute timestamp, which fails the time-attribution obligation of RQ-003, and its content is cleared by a cold restart, which fails the retention obligation of RQ-012. A structured fuzzing run additionally surfaced a temporary, self-recovering disruption of the HTTP service that requires follow-up by development. Because no requirement reached a full pass verdict in the bounded practical phase, the Requirement Compliance Rate is $RCR = 0$, and the Test Effectiveness Ratio over the executed subset is $TER = 2/15 \approx 0.13$. Almost every executed activity is backed by stored raw evidence.

With respect to the research questions, RQ1 is answered by the traceable derivation chain and the two-level catalogue, which turn regulatory obligations into concrete, requirement-linked tests. RQ2 is answered by the applicability assessment, which identified the non-applicable and blocked tests before execution and thereby prevented four tools from being run against interfaces that the device does not expose. RQ3 is answered in part. NV1 produced usable evidence for the HTTP interface, the identification surface, the intervention log, the application-layer availability, and the robustness of the HTTP service, but it cannot verify any property that depends on the cyclic safety communication, which requires the passive-observation or the in-path variant.

The results contribute to the goal, but the contribution is methodological rather than a completed conformity assessment. The methodology closed the process gap for the examined device by producing traceable evidence that links each executed test to a requirement, which is the property that the technical documentation under the regulation requires. The applicability assessment proved to be a necessary step, because a general-purpose IT test framework cannot be applied unchanged to an embedded safety gateway. The work also made a conceptual gap visible. The manufacturer argues that a security fault resolves into a safe state, whereas the regulation demands security properties such as integrity, accountability, confidentiality, and availability as ends in themselves. The methodology therefore examines empirically the difference between a device that fails safely and a device that is secure, and the two intervention-log deviations are a concrete example of that difference. The derivation also exposed a gap in the normative basis itself, for example RQ-013, for which prEN 50742 provides no corresponding clause.

The methodology addresses reliability at three levels. Repeatability is supported by the two-level test specification, which records the exact procedure and evidence for each test. Reproducibility is supported by the use of a standardised tool environment and by the applicability assessment, which documents why a tool applies to the target. Traceability is supported by the requirement chain of Section \ref{subsec:traceability_model}.

Several factors limit the validity of the results. The evaluation covers a single System Under Test, so the results do not generalise to other devices or fieldbuses. The practical demonstration is limited to the NV1 network position, so claims that depend on passive observation or active manipulation remain unverified. The cyclic safety communication is not independently monitored, so the methodology does not claim that the safety function is unaffected by a test. prEN 50742 is a draft standard, so the derivation supports the demonstration of conformity without asserting certification.

For these reasons, the artifact is best described as a prototype methodology with partial practical validation. The methodology is fully specified across all three network access variants, and it is demonstrated for the tests that the NV1 position supports. The validation of the NV2 and NV3 tests remains future work.

\section{Future Work} 
\label{sec:future_work}
%TODO
Several directions extend this work. The most immediate is to realise the two remaining network access variants. The passive-observation variant NV2, using a mirroring switch or a network tap, and the in-path variant NV3, using a transparent bridge, would allow the safety-telegram integrity, the in-path manipulation, the watchdog-passivation, and the domain-separation scenarios to be executed. This would in turn allow the requirements that depend on the cyclic safety communication, in particular RQ-002 and RQ-005, to be assessed. The outstanding configuration-change and firmware tests should also be added to complete the requirement coverage.

Because prEN 50742 grades the required test depth through the Safety-Related Security Level, the catalogue can be extended so that the strictness of a test scales with the level of the affected asset and requirement, for example from a simple checksum check at a low level to cryptographic verification under active manipulation at a high level.

A device that exposes an authenticated and encrypted interface, for example over HTTPS, would enable the credential, transport-security, and access-control tests that were not applicable to the current device, and would raise the achievable requirement coverage.

The methodology should be embedded permanently in the development and quality-management process. This includes assigning the responsibilities of the governance model, fixing the point in the lifecycle at which tests are generated and evaluated, and maintaining the traceability matrix when the normative basis changes, for example when prEN 50742 is published in its final form, so that the methodology can serve as conformity documentation under the Machinery Regulation. A related open point is the precise regulatory scope for a component that is not itself a machine, because the Safe Remote I/O is a component whose obligations arise through its integration into a machine.

Finally, the use of machine learning is a promising direction for security testing. A profiling module that learns the normal network behaviour of a device in the testbed could support the detection of anomalous behaviour during a test \cite[pp. 12]{siboni2016securitytestbedinternetthings}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The results address the scientific problem stated in Chapter \ref{chap:introduction}, the absence of a systematic approach for verifying security functions at the IT/OT interface. The methodology narrowed this gap for the examined device by producing traceable evidence that links each executed test to a requirement.

The company framework could not be adopted without adaptation. Its instructions assume interfaces that a general IT target exposes, such as an authentication endpoint, a TLS endpoint, or an operating-system shell. The embedded SUT exposes none of these. The applicability assessment made this visible before execution and prevented three tools from being run against a target they do not fit. This confirms that a tool-to-target applicability step is a necessary part of the methodology, not an optional refinement. The non-applicability of CM-002, CM-003, CM-005, and CM-006 is therefore a methodological result, not a product test failure.

The requirement-oriented test cases improved traceability over the tool-oriented company instructions. Each executed activity mapped to a requirement and to a stored artifact, which is the property that the regulation requires for technical documentation. The two-level specification allowed one practical activity, such as the Nmap run or the identification query, to serve several requirements without duplicating the execution.

The NV1 position produced usable evidence for the HTTP interface, the identification surface, the intervention log, the application-layer availability, and the robustness of the HTTP service. It could not produce evidence about the cyclic safety communication, which would require the NV2 or NV3 position. The clearest empirical results are the time-attribution and the cold-start-deletion findings on the intervention log, because they rest on a controlled, repeatable read sequence with stored raw data.

Existing functional and safety tests can contribute to the methodology where the security objective is explicit and the acceptance criterion is suitable. The PROFIsafe passivation behaviour, for example, is validated for random faults and could be reused for the corresponding security objective after the acceptance criterion is extended to a deliberately induced disruption. Functional safety validation does not, on its own, verify cybersecurity, because it does not include an adversarial perspective.

\section{Reliability and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:reliability_validity}
The reliability of the findings varies by test. The intervention-log tests are the most reliable, because the initial state, the trigger, the tool, the command, and the raw output are documented, so another tester could repeat them. The scan and identification tests are reproducible from the stored commands and outputs, including the tool versions Nmap 7.99 and SSLscan 2.1.5. The boofuzz robustness run is documented by stored session databases and a run log. The Siege load runs are stored and show a degraded availability under load, and the connection-hold and layer-two flood runs are documented by stored output. The write, configuration, and firmware tests are only partly covered or not executed, so their reproducibility is limited until the evidence is completed.

The construct validity of individual tests is bounded. A generic scanner does not always measure the intended product property. The Nikto Spring Boot Actuator finding illustrates this, because a generic signature can misclassify the IoT-Core JSON. An application-layer load test does not measure the resilience of the safety channel. Visible version information is an identification surface, not a complete software inventory.

The internal validity is bounded by open causation and by state dependence. The cause of the error 0x1000 is unresolved. The order of tests can affect the device state, because the log content depends on prior events and on the last cold restart. The error-log entries carry only a relative system tick, which prevents correlation across a power cycle.

The external validity is bounded by the single case. One device, one configuration, and one topology were evaluated, and only NV1 was implemented. The results do not transfer directly to other products or fieldbuses. The methodology, however, is device-independent and can be re-applied to other components.

The tool reliability is bounded by the embedded target. Generic security tools assume a general IT host and can produce false positives or incomplete coverage on an embedded device. The GVM/OpenVAS scanner could not be brought into operation in the laboratory, so the known-CVE status remains open. These threats are addressed by the applicability assessment and by the requirement that scanner findings be manually validated before they are reported as results.
