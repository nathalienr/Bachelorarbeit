\chapter{Methodology} 
\label{chap:methodology} 

This chapter describes the methodological approach used to develop and evaluate the proposed security testing framework. Building upon the theoretical foundations presented in Chapter \ref{chap:fundamentals}, the research follows the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) proposed by \citeauthor{Peffers} DSRM provides a structured process for addressing practical problems through the design, development, demonstration, and evaluation of artifacts \cite[p. 46]{Peffers}. The objective of this research is the development of a standards-based functional security testing framework for industrial gateways. The framework is intended to enable the traceable, repeatable, and systematic verification of security functions derived from the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, prEN 50742, and the IEC 62443 series. Within the scope of this thesis, the activities of the DSRM process are mapped to the corresponding chapters. Problem identification and objective definition are presented in Chapter \ref{chap:introduction}. The design and development phase is addressed in this chapter through the derivation of security requirements and the development of the proposed testing framework. The demonstration and evaluation phases are conducted in Chapter \ref{chap:implementation_results_evaluation}, where the framework is applied to an industrial gateway as a case study. The communication phase is represented by the documentation, discussion, and dissemination of the research results through this thesis.

The requirements developed in this section use the MVO cybersecurity requirements contained in \texttt{table \ref{tab:mvo-requirements}} (RQ-001 to RQ-023, derived from Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, Annex III) as the authoritative starting point. The STRIDE-based threat modelling, attack-potential (AP) calculation, and Security-Related Security Level (SRSL) determination methodology already defined in prEN 50742 (Annex B and Annex C) are referenced only as the interpretive framework of that standard and are not re-executed.

prEN 50742 is used exclusively as a bridging/interpretation standard: it operationalises the Machinery Regulation's essential health and safety requirements on protection against corruption (Annex III, Sections 1.1.9 and 1.2.1) into concrete, testable product requirements (Section 7, ``Approach A'') and, alternatively, into references to EN IEC 62443-3-3 and EN IEC 62443-4-2 (Section 8, ``Approach B''). Annex ZZ of prEN 50742 formally confirms this bridging role by mapping its own Sections 4.3, 7.17.5, 8.3 and 9 back to Annex III, Sections 1.1.9 and 1.2.1 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230.

Each MVO requirement is mapped below to the corresponding prEN 50742 clause(s), concept(s), or Security-Related Security Level (SRSL) tier. Where prEN 50742 provides SRSL-tiered obligations (SRSL0 to SRSL3), these are preserved as separate, atomic, SRSL-conditioned requirements rather than being merged, since each tier is independently verifiable and the applicable tier is determined per security function by the manufacturer's own SRSL assessment (prEN 50742, 7.4.2), which is not repeated here. Where prEN 50742 explicitly excludes a topic from its scope (functional safety requirements of control systems; see prEN 50742, Section 1, Note 2) or provides no corresponding clause, the mapping is marked ``No direct mapping identified'' or ``Partial mapping'' with a brief explanation, and the original MVO requirement is retained unchanged as the residual authoritative source for that obligation.

The result is a refined, standards-contextualised requirement baseline (pREN-RQ-001 to pREN-RQ-031) intended to support functional security testing of industrial gateways operating at the IT/OT boundary, and to serve as an intermediate artefact for the subsequent mapping step to IEC 62443-4-2 component requirements (CR/EDR).

Each row records the mapping type between the MVO requirement and prEN 50742:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Direct} -- prEN 50742 contains a clause that expresses essentially the same obligation.
	\item \textbf{Partial} -- prEN 50742 covers part of the obligation, but scope, trigger condition, or wording differs.
	\item \textbf{Indirect} -- prEN 50742 addresses a related concept or general principle, without a clause-level match.
	\item \textbf{Not identified} -- no supporting clause could be found in prEN\_50742\_source.txt; the mapping is a confirmed gap (or, where noted, a conflict).
\end{itemize}

\section{Functional Security Testing Framework Architecture}
\label{sec:testing_methodology_development} 


Problem aktueller Methodiken: 
- klassischer Funktionale Ansatz (Black-Box-Ansatz): Prüft nur Soll-Verhalten, Keine Aussage über Angriffswiderstand > Für Nachweisführung regulatorischer Anforderungen allein nicht ausreichend
- Security Verificaton nach IEC 62443: Abstrakt, liefert keine konkreten Testfälle > bietet aber Grundlage für die Ableitung systematischer Testfälle
- Penetration Testing: Geringe Reproduzierbarkeit, Ergebnis abhängig vom Tester >  Für die Maschinenverordnung liefern Penetration Tests starke Evidenz, ersetzen jedoch keine strukturierte Verifikationsmethodik.
- Fuzz Testing: Erfordert spezialisiertes Testsetup, schwierige Ergebnisanalyse > geeignet für Prüfung robuster Kommunikation und Fehlerbehandlung nach IEC 62443
- Protocol Security Testing
- OWASP IoT Security Testing Guide: nicht speziell für OT entwickelt
- Risk-based Security Testing: Aufwendige Riskoanalyse notwendig

In the context of industrial gateways, which act as critical boundaries at the IT/OT interface, these 
devices are primarily evaluated under the stringent requirements for network components.


Funktionale Tests prüfen, ob das System tut, was es soll. Security Tests prüfen, ob das System auch das unterlässt, was es nicht tun darf.

Deswegen kombiniertes Testframework:

1. Risk Assessment / Threat Modelling
2. Ableitung von Security Requirements
3. Funktionale Security Tests
4. Protocol Security Tests
5. Fuzz Testing
6. Gezielte Penetration Tests
7. Compliance-Mapping auf EU-Maschinenverordnung und IEC 62443
